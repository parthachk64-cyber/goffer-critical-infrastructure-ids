# Results

Two runs are committed under `results/`. Every number is **measured in this run** by this repository's code, with seed 42, and can be regenerated by the commands in the README. Each
run directory's `report.md` is the authoritative copy: it is rebuilt from that run's `predictions.csv` and `manifest.json`.
Metric definitions: positive class = label 1 (attack); precision, recall, F1, FPR and accuracy are computed at each model's documented threshold; a ratio with a zero denominator is reported as `n/a`, never as 0.

## 1. Independent benchmark: UNSW-NB15 official partitions

Directory: `results/unsw-nb15-example/` (summary files; `predictions.csv` is not committed because of its size, so the report regenerates after you run `benchmark`).
Inputs: `UNSW_NB15_training-set.csv` (175,341 rows; SHA-256 `bec7dd5e...`) and `UNSW_NB15_testing-set.csv` (82,332 rows; SHA-256 `734fe664...`). Fit 140,272 / validation 35,069 rows from the training file.

| model | threshold | TP | FP | TN | FN | accuracy | precision | recall | F1 | FPR | AP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| majority reference | 0.500 | 45,332 | 37,000 | 0 | 0 | 0.551 | 0.551 | 1.000 | 0.710 | 1.000 | n/a |
| rule baseline | 0.500 | 29,727 | 6,508 | 30,492 | 15,605 | 0.731 | 0.820 | 0.656 | 0.729 | 0.176 | n/a |
| decision tree | 0.484 | 44,130 | 11,153 | 25,847 | 1,202 | 0.850 | 0.798 | 0.974 | 0.877 | 0.301 | 0.977 |
| isolation forest | 0.734 | 38,454 | 13,888 | 23,112 | 6,878 | 0.748 | 0.735 | 0.848 | 0.787 | 0.375 | 0.801 |
| MLP | 0.487 | 44,635 | 10,089 | 26,911 | 697 | 0.869 | 0.816 | 0.985 | 0.892 | 0.273 | 0.985 |
| isolation forest + rule | 0.732 | 38,559 | 14,196 | 22,804 | 6,773 | 0.745 | 0.731 | 0.851 | 0.786 | 0.384 | 0.816 |

What it shows, and what it does not:

- The two supervised models separate the classes best (AP 0.977 and 0.985) but still raise 27% to 30% false positives at the F1-optimal threshold. The tree's and MLP's advantage over the unsupervised detector is expected: they see attack labels, the Isolation Forest does not.
- **Adding the rule to the same Isolation Forest barely moved anything**: F1 0.787 to 0.786, FPR 0.375 to 0.384, AP 0.801 to 0.816. The article claims hybrid systems cut false alarms (p.1684). On this data and this rule, false positives did not fall. This is one rule and one fusion weight; it is a measured negative result for that choice, not evidence about hybrids in general.
- The majority reference reaches F1 0.710 only because attacks are the majority (55%) in this partition; read F1 against it, and prefer AP and FPR.
- **Duplicates.** 8,542 of 82,332 test rows (10.4%) have an exact model-input duplicate in the training file, 524 of them with a conflicting label, and 84,049 training rows are internal duplicates. Excluding the overlapping test rows, F1 falls slightly (tree 0.877 to 0.862, MLP 0.892 to 0.879, isolation forest 0.787 to 0.765). So part of the headline is memorisation of repeated rows; the full table is in the report.
- Attack-category recall is available from `predictions.csv` (`attack_cat` is evaluation-only) but is not interpreted here.
- Inference latency (batch of 256 rows, 5 warm-up, 30 repetitions, Windows 11, 12 logical CPUs, Python 3.12): p50 per batch about 0.7 ms (tree), 5 ms (MLP), 6 ms (rule), 255 ms (Isolation Forest), 45 ms preprocessing. Timings vary between runs and machines and exclude any simulated analyst delay.

## 2. Synthetic demonstration (not real traffic)

Directory: `results/synthetic-demo-example/`. Seeded generator, 2,000 training and 1,000 test rows with four scenarios including two ambiguous ones. The signal is built in, so this verifies the pipeline, not detection ability.

| model | TP | FP | TN | FN | precision | recall | F1 | FPR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| majority reference | 0 | 0 | 650 | 350 | n/a | 0.000 | n/a | 0.000 |
| rule baseline | 253 | 84 | 566 | 97 | 0.751 | 0.723 | 0.737 | 0.129 |
| decision tree | 259 | 27 | 623 | 91 | 0.906 | 0.740 | 0.815 | 0.042 |
| isolation forest | 240 | 72 | 578 | 110 | 0.769 | 0.686 | 0.725 | 0.111 |
| MLP | 241 | 10 | 640 | 109 | 0.960 | 0.689 | 0.802 | 0.015 |
| isolation forest + rule | 245 | 70 | 580 | 105 | 0.778 | 0.700 | 0.737 | 0.108 |

On the synthetic set the rule gave a small improvement to the Isolation Forest (F1 0.725 to 0.737); on UNSW-NB15 it gave none. Both are single seeds with no confidence intervals.

## Triage and disposition

Both runs write `alerts.jsonl` (event id, model version, risk score, threshold, reason codes, proposed action) and `queue.jsonl` (open, reviewed, dismissed or escalated with timestamps and reasons).
The dispositions are **simulated** by a policy that reads only alert content (escalate if a rule reason code is present, else dismiss) on a simulated clock. They are not analyst behaviour, and the simulated delays are not response times.
The benchmark alert queue is capped at 5,000 of 52,755 flagged rows (highest risk first) and the report states the truncation.

## Claims of the article that these results can and cannot address

| Article claim | Status |
|---|---|
| ML/IDS detect threats with useful precision and recall (p.1667) | Evaluated on one public flow benchmark only; the article reports no numbers to compare with |
| Hybrid AI plus rules reduces false alarms (p.1684) | Tested for one rule and one fusion formula: no reduction on UNSW-NB15 (measured), small gain on the synthetic fixture |
| Deep models handle complex threats best (p.1683) | A small MLP was best here on both AP and F1; CNN and RNN were not tested |
| AI SOCs respond faster, with fewer human errors (p.1683) | Unavailable: needs deployment logs and event-onset/disposition timestamps. Not measured |
| Interview findings, sector case outcomes | Unavailable in source |

Public network-flow scores do not extrapolate to national-security outcomes.
